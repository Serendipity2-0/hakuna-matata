    GST Process

    A) Eligibility

    Businesses providing services with an annual turnover exceeding ₹20 lakh are required to register for GST. For special category states, this limit is ₹10 lakh.

    GST Eligibility Information

    B) Documents Required

    1. PAN Card of the proprietor.
    2. Aadhaar Card of the proprietor.
    3. Proof of Business Registration: Certificate of Incorporation or business registration certificate.
    4. Address Proof of Principal Place of Business: Electricity bill, rent agreement, or property tax receipt.
    5. Bank Account Details: Cancelled cheque, bank statement, or the first page of the passbook.
    6. Photograph of the proprietor.

    C) Portal for Registration

    GST Registration Portal

    D) New Registration Steps

    • Select 'Taxpayer' as the type of taxpayer.
    • State and District: Choose the appropriate state and district where your business is located.
    • Legal Name of the Business: Enter the name as per your PAN.
    • PAN: Input your Permanent Account Number.
    • Email Address and Mobile Number: Provide valid contact details for OTP verification.
    • Proceed: After entering the details, click on 'Proceed'.

    E) OTP Verification

    Enter the OTPs sent to your registered email and mobile number.

    F) Temporary Reference Number

    Upon successful verification, you'll receive a TRN. Note this number for future reference.

    G) Filling Application Form

    • Log in with TRN: Return to the GST portal, select 'Temporary Reference Number (TRN)', and log in using the TRN and captcha code.
    • Business Details: Provide information about your business, including trade name, constitution, and commencement date.
    • Promoter/Partner Details: Enter personal information, designation, and contact details of the proprietor.
    • Authorized Signatory: If you wish to appoint someone else as an authorized signatory, provide their details.
    • Principal Place of Business: Input the address and contact information of your main business location.
    • Additional Places of Business: If applicable, provide details of other business locations.
    • Goods and Services: Specify the services you offer along with their HSN/SAC codes.
    • Bank Account Details: Enter your bank account information.
    • Verification: Review the application, select the verification checkbox, and submit using one of the following methods:
    • Digital Signature Certificate (DSC): Mandatory for companies and LLPs.
    • Electronic Verification Code (EVC): OTP sent to registered mobile number.
    • e-Sign: OTP sent to Aadhaar-registered mobile number.

    H) Application Reference Number (ARN)

    After submission, you'll receive an ARN via email and SMS.

    I) Application Processing and Allotment

    The GST authorities will process your application. If additional information is required, you'll be notified. Upon approval, you'll receive a 15-digit GST Identification Number (GSTIN) and a GST registration certificate.

    GST Collection

    Issuing Tax Invoices

    For every taxable supply of services, issue a tax invoice containing: - Your GSTIN

    • Invoice number and date

    • Customer's details

    • Description of services provided

    • Value of services

    • Applicable GST rates and amounts (CGST, SGST/UTGST, or IGST)

    • Total invoice value

    Charging GST

    Apply the appropriate GST rate to your services. For web hosting, website development, and application development, the standard GST rate is 18%.

    GST Accounting

    Utilizing Input Tax Credit (ITC)

    Offset the GST paid on purchases (input tax) against the GST collected on sales (output tax). Ensure that ITC is claimed only on eligible inputs and that all conditions for claiming ITC are met.

    GST Filing

    Types of GST Returns and Due Dates

    • GSTR-1: Monthly or quarterly return detailing outward supplies (sales).
    • Monthly Filing: Due on the 11th of the following month.
    • Quarterly Filing (for turnover up to ₹5 crore): Due on the 13th of the month following the quarter.
    • GSTR-3B: Monthly summary return of outward and inward supplies, input tax credit, and tax liability.
    • Monthly Filing: Due on the 20th of the following month.
    • Quarterly Filing (under QRMP scheme): Due on the 22nd or 24th of the month following the quarter, depending on the state.
    • GSTR-9: Annual return summarizing the financial year's GST activities.
    • Due Date: 31st December following the end of the financial year.

    Late Fees and Penalties

    • Late Filing Fees:
    • GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B: ₹50 per day of delay (₹25 CGST + ₹25 SGST), capped at ₹5,000.
    • GSTR-9: ₹200 per day of delay (₹100 CGST + ₹100 SGST), subject to a maximum of 0.25% of turnover.
    • Interest on Late Payment: 18% per annum on the outstanding tax amount, calculated from the due date until the actual payment date.

    Filing Process

    • Access the GST Portal: Log in to the GST portal using your credentials.
    • Prepare Returns:
    • GSTR-1: Upload details of outward supplies, including invoice-wise details of B2B transactions and aggregate details of B2C transactions.
    • GSTR-3B: Provide summary details of outward supplies, input tax credit, and tax liability.
    • Verify and Submit: Review the information for accuracy, submit the return, and file using DSC (Digital Signature Certificate) or EVC (Electronic Verification Code).
    • Make Payment: Pay any tax liability through net banking, credit/debit card, or other authorized payment modes.

    Serendipity Under GST Filing and Process

    For web hosting, website development, and application development services, these services typically fall under the "Information Technology and Software Services" category. Under GST, web-related services, including hosting, development, and other IT services, are subject to an 18% GST rate.

    1. Classification of Web Services Under GST

    • HSN/SAC Code: For IT and software-related services, the SAC (Services Accounting Code) is typically 9983 or 9984, depending on the exact service nature.
    • GST Rate: The standard GST rate for IT, software, and web services, including web hosting, is 18% (9% CGST + 9% SGST for intra-state or 18% IGST for inter-state).

    2. Provisions and Discounts on GST Payable

    • Discounts and Adjustments: In GST filings, any discounts given to customers can be adjusted under certain conditions:
    • Pre-supply Discounts: If the discount is mentioned in the invoice, GST is only calculated on the reduced amount after the discount.
    • Post-supply Discounts: If discounts are offered after the supply, they may also be deducted from the tax value provided that:
      • The discount is established in terms of an agreement before the supply.
      • Input Tax Credit (ITC) attributable to the discount is reversed by the recipient.
    • Promotional Discounts: Include these details in the contract or invoice to ensure GST compliance.

    3. Other Applicable Provisions

    • Input Tax Credit (ITC): Claim ITC on business expenses (e.g., office rent, software purchases, cloud services) to offset your GST payable. Ensure vendor compliance for ITC eligibility.
    • Reduced Tax for Export of Services: If clients are located outside India, you may qualify for zero-rated GST on export services. This requires GST registration and export documentation for zero tax or claim refunds on input taxes.

    4. Filing Details

    • Invoicing Requirements: When offering discounts, clearly mention these on the invoices to ensure GST calculations are based on the reduced amounts.
    • Record-Keeping for Discounts and Refunds: Maintain agreements, credit notes, and invoices to support any post-supply discounts or refunds claimed on GST returns.

    Can GST Credit Be Used to Offset Business Losses?

    Unfortunately, GST and Income Tax are separate systems with no provision allowing GST credit to directly offset income tax losses. Here’s why: - GST ITC (Input Tax Credit) can only be used to offset GST payable on sales or services. It cannot reduce your income tax payable or business losses. - GST Collected vs. Business Profit/Loss: GST collected on sales is considered an indirect tax liability that doesn’t affect your profit or loss for income tax purposes. Therefore, business losses and GST credits are accounted for independently.
