## Summary Table:

| Aspect                   | Capital Expenditure                   | Revenue Expenditure                   |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Definition**           | Long-term investments in assets       | Day-to-day expenses                   |
| **Purpose**              | Asset acquisition or enhancement      | Operational expenses                  |
| **Accounting Treatment** | Capitalized and depreciated over time | Fully expensed in the year incurred   |
| **Examples**             | Machinery, buildings, renovations     | Salaries, rent, maintenance           |
| **Tax Treatment**        | Depreciation deductible over time     | Fully deductible in the year incurred |
| **Frequency**            | Infrequent                            | Frequent and ongoing                  |
| **Benefit Duration**     | Long-term                             | Short-term, within the year           |

### 1. Definition

- **Capital Expenditure (CapEx)**: This is the spending on assets or improvements to assets that will benefit the business over multiple years. These expenses create future benefits and typically relate to major investments in fixed assets.
- **Revenue Expenditure (RevEx)**: These are expenses incurred during regular business operations, primarily for maintaining current assets or covering day-to-day expenses. They do not create long-term value but are essential for running the business.

### 2. Purpose

- **Capital Expenditure**: Aimed at acquiring or upgrading physical assets (e.g., property, equipment) to improve business efficiency or capacity.
- **Revenue Expenditure**: Primarily incurred to ensure smooth business operations and maintain existing assets rather than expanding or improving the business.

### 3. Accounting Treatment

- **Capital Expenditure**: Recorded as an asset on the balance sheet. The expense is not fully deducted in the year of purchase but is spread over the asset’s useful life through depreciation.
- **Revenue Expenditure**: Immediately recorded as an expense on the income statement for the period in which it is incurred. It directly impacts the profit or loss of the business for that year.

### 4. Examples

- **Capital Expenditure**:
  o Purchase of machinery, equipment, buildings, or land

o Renovation or improvement of facilities

o Installation costs for new assets

o Vehicles or other long-term investments

- **Revenue Expenditure**:
  o Salaries and wages

o Rent and utilities

o Maintenance and repair costs

o Office supplies, advertising, and marketing expenses

o Interest on loans for daily business operations

### 5. Impact on Taxation

- **Capital Expenditure**: Not fully deductible in the year incurred. Instead, it is capitalized and depreciated over time. Depreciation deductions help reduce taxable income over the asset’s useful life.
- **Revenue Expenditure**: Fully deductible as an expense in the year incurred, reducing taxable income for that period.

### 6. Frequency

- **Capital Expenditure**: Occurs infrequently or irregularly, as it involves significant investments.
- **Revenue Expenditure**: Occurs regularly and is ongoing as part of daily business operations.

### 7. Benefit Duration

- **Capital Expenditure**: Provides benefits to the business over several years.
- **Revenue Expenditure**: Provides benefits only within the current accounting period.

## 1. Income Tax Return (ITR) Filing Due Dates for Proprietorships:

The due dates for filing ITR depend on whether the proprietorship requires an audit

- **Non-Audit Cases**: If the proprietorship's accounts do not require an audit, the due date for filing the ITR for the Financial Year (FY) 2023-24 (Assessment Year 2024-25) was 31st July 2024.
  [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/due-date-tax-filing)

- **Audit Cases**: For proprietorships requiring an audit, the due date was initially 31st October 2024. However, the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) extended this deadline to 15th November 2024.
  [CA Club India](https://www.caclubindia.com/news/cbdt-extends-due-date-for-audited-itr-returns-for-ay-2024-25-to-15th-nov-2024-24044.asp)

## 2. Penalties for Late Filing of ITR:

Filing the ITR after the due date attracts penalties under Section 234F of the Income Tax Act:

- **If Filed After the Due Date but on or Before 31st December 2024:**

  o Total Income Above ₹5 Lakhs: Penalty of ₹5,000.

  o Total Income Up to ₹5 Lakhs: Penalty of ₹1,000.

[ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/belated-return-not-filed-your-income-tax-return)

- **If Filed After 31st December 2024:**

  o Filing beyond this date is not permitted for the relevant assessment year.

## 3. Interest on Tax Due for Late Filing:

In addition to the penalty, interest is levied under Section 234A for late filing:

- **Interest Rate**: 1% per month or part of a month on the outstanding tax amount, calculated from the original due date until the actual filing date.

[Tax2Win](https://tax2win.in/guide/penalty-for-late-filing-of-income-tax-return)

## 4. Consequences of Not Filing the ITR:

- **Failing to file the ITR can lead to**:

  • **Prosecution**: In cases of willful default, prosecution may result in imprisonment ranging from 3 months to 2 years, along with a fine.

  • **Loss of Carry Forward Benefits**: Losses incurred (except house property loss) cannot be carried forward to subsequent years if the ITR is not filed within the due date.

##5. Short Payment or Non-Payment of Tax:

- **If there is a shortfall in tax payment**:

  • Interest Under Section 234B: Interest at 1% per month is levied for default in payment of advance tax.

  • Interest Under Section 234C: Interest at 1% per month is levied for deferment of advance tax installments.
