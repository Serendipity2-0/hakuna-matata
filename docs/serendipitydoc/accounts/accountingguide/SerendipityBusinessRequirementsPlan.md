## Important Pointers

- **Business Income and Expenditure**: Since the business expenditure is much higher than income, Omkar’s firm shows a net loss for the year. This loss can be reported under Income from Business and Profession.
- **Interest Paid on Loans**: The interest paid on loans borrowed from friends, family, and others for business purposes can be claimed as a business expense, reducing the taxable income or increasing the reported loss.
- **Depreciation on Assets**: If most of the 2 crore rupees was spent on acquiring business assets, Omkar can claim depreciation, which further offsets business income.
- **Carrying Forward Losses**: Since the business is operating at a loss, Omkar can carry forward these business losses for up to eight years and set them off against future business income. However, the losses need to be filed within the due date to be eligible for this carryforward.

### Scenario 1 - Carry Forward and Set Off of Business Losses

- **Section 72 Income Tax Act - Carry Forward and Set Off of Business Losses**:
   - Under Section 72, losses from a business (excluding losses from speculative business) can be carried forward for up to 8 assessment years immediately following the assessment year in which the loss was incurred.
   - These carried-forward losses can only be set off against business income in future years. For example, if Omkar’s business shows a profit in a future year, the previously incurred losses can be used to offset that profit, reducing the taxable income.
- **Conditions for Carrying Forward**:
   - Losses can only be carried forward if the income tax return reporting these losses is filed by the due date.
   - The business for which the losses are carried forward must be continued in the year the loss is set off. This means that if Omkar’s business closes, the ability to set off these losses against future profits is lost.
- **Set-Off Provisions for Interest and Depreciation Expenses**:
   - Expenses like interest on loans and depreciation on assets reduce the business income for the current year, and any residual loss after these deductions can be carried forward under Section 72.

   Link for above Scenario - [Section 72 - Carry Forward and Set Off of Business Losses](https://cleartax.in/v/it/income-tax-acts-sections/section-72-carry-forward-and-set-off-of-business-losses-income-tax-act-1961-1965)

### Scenario 2 - Belated filing of ITR

- **Belated Return Filing Deadline**
   - Section 139(4) of the Income Tax Act, 1961: This section allows taxpayers to file a belated return for a previous financial year up to December 31 of the assessment year, following the end of the financial year.
   - Source: Income Tax Act - Section 139(4)
- **Penalties for Late Filing**
   - Section 234F: This section outlines the penalty for filing a belated return, with penalties based on income thresholds and timing.
   - Section 234A, 234B, and 234C: These sections cover interest on unpaid tax for late filings.
   - Source: Income Tax Act - Sections 234F, 234A, 234B, and 234C
- **Document Preparation for Filing**
   - The Income Tax Department’s guidelines recommend maintaining accurate records of income, expenses, assets, loans, and other tax-relevant documents.
   - Source: Income Tax Filing - Document Checklist (search for ‘ITR filing document checklist’)
- **Loss Carry Forward and Set-Off**
   - Section 72 of the Income Tax Act, 1961: This section covers the carry forward of business losses and sets conditions for setting off these losses against future business income.
   - Source: Income Tax Act - Section 72
- **Filing Process for Belated Return**
   - The Income Tax Department provides a comprehensive guide for using the e-filing portal to file both timely and belated returns.
   - Source: Income Tax e-filing Portal Guide
- **Verification of Return**
   - As per Rule 12 of the Income Tax Rules, 1962, and Income Tax guidelines, every filed ITR needs verification through one of the approved methods (Aadhaar OTP, net banking, EVC, etc.) for it to be considered valid.
   - Source: Income Tax e-filing Verification Process
- **Revised Return for Corrections**
   - Section 139(5) of the Income Tax Act, 1961: This section allows a taxpayer to revise an ITR to correct errors, provided it’s done within the filing deadline (usually December 31 of the assessment year).
   - Source: Income Tax Act - Section 139(5)

### Scenario 3 - TDS on Salary

- **Section 192 - TDS on Salary**
   - Section 192 mandates that employers deduct TDS from salaries if the employee’s income exceeds the basic exemption limit. The TDS amount depends on the employee’s income tax slab for the financial year.
   - Source: Income Tax Act - Section 192 (search “Section 192” for details on TDS on salaries).
- **Section 201(1A) - Interest on Delayed TDS Deduction and Payment**
   - Section 201(1A) specifies interest for delayed TDS: 1% per month for late deduction and 1.5% per month for late payment after deduction.
   - Source: Income Tax Act - Section 201(1A) (search “Section 201” for interest calculations).
- **Section 271C - Penalty for Failure to Deduct TDS**
   - Section 271C addresses penalties for failure to deduct TDS, which can equal the amount of TDS that was not deducted.
   - Source: Income Tax Act - Section 271C (search “Section 271C” for penalty details).
- **TDS Return Filing Requirements**
   - The Income Tax Department requires TDS to be filed quarterly via Form 24Q for salary deductions. The belated or revised filing can be done through the Income Tax e-filing Portal.
   - Source: Income Tax e-filing Portal Guide (search for “Form 24Q” for TDS on salaries return filing).
